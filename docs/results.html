        Trades both sides gain from

        Every package of up to two players a side was enumerated against all eleven leaguemates, keeping only those where both starting lineups improve: —. In a pure-projection lens a fair trade is near zero-sum, so the survivors are exactly the positional-surplus mismatches: KGB's spare running backs against someone else's spare receiver.

        The five moves

        1. Sell the fourth and fifth running back, buy a receiver.

          Lloyd (RB28) and Price (RB24) are startable for teams whose RB2 is a committee back, and worth nothing to a team already starting McCaffrey and Hampton. Every trade in the table above is a version of that swap. Lead with David, Win95 and Keung — the three whose receiver room is deep enough to sell from.

        2. Do not pay for a quarterback.

          Replacement QB in this pool projects 379; Mahomes projects 385. The entire positional edge is six points a season. Mahomes at 11.07 was a correct read, and the corollary is that any offer sending real skill-position value the other way for a "better" QB is a loss. If someone offers a receiver for Mahomes, take it and stream.

        3. Drop Kyler Murray.

          QB24 behind an every-week QB1 is a roster spot spending itself. Three teams carry a single quarterback — Sly, Win95 and TheOhZone — but only TheOhZone's is weak enough to want help, and Shough-for-Murray is not a trade anyone wins. Ask once, then cut him for a receiver flyer.

        4. Cut the second defense, Rodriguez and Kamara — then stream.

          Four roster spots return either nothing or less than nothing (Rodriguez −48, Kamara −75 against replacement). Keep the Buccaneers, stream the matchup weekly, and spend the freed spots on receiver upside — which is the only position where this roster has room to gain.

        5. Leave the kicker alone.

          Mevis is K9 and the spread from K1 to K12 is about 30 points. It is the one position where the correct amount of in-season effort is zero.

        Method & caveats. Replacement level is the last league-wide starter at each position for a QB/2RB/3WR/TE/FLEX/K lineup, with the FLEX slot split half RB, half WR — . Value over replacement is a player's projected points minus that level, and a team's score is the sum over its best legal starting lineup. An unfillable slot is charged the full replacement level, so trading away your only quarterback is never free.

        • Defenses are excluded. The source file projects every DST between −69 and −190 points, which is a different scale rather than a ranking, so mixing it into a points total would be meaningless. DSTs still appear on rosters.
        • Projections are the tracker's, not this model's. Ranks and projected points come from the draft export as-is; a few disagree with each other (a player ranked ahead of another at the same position while projecting fewer points). Where they conflict, the points column drives everything here.
        • Season-long points only. No bye conflicts, injury risk, playoff schedule, or week-to-week variance — a two-point edge in this table is noise, a fifty-point edge is not.
        • Trade gains are the projection's view, not a negotiation. A leaguemate who is higher on his own player will say no regardless of what the arithmetic says.
