Slot comparison — is a better slot worth anything?

Each strategy's mean projected starting-lineup points, by slot; the rule behind each dot is the 10th–90th percentile across 4,000 drafts. The whole spread across slots is about 12 points — under a point per week, and it is dwarfed by the run-to-run spread, so slot choice matters far less than the round-2 tier cliff below.

    Why slot 5 wins — and why it barely does

    Expected value of the best player available at your first two picks. Moving from slot 5 back to slot 7 gains you only a little in round 2 but costs much more in round 1: the snake's turn-back does not repay the round-1 loss, because round 1 is where the steep tier cliffs sit.

      The tier cliffs

      Value over replacement for the top of each position, shaded by tier. The gaps are the point: RB has a two-man top tier, and TE falls 49 points after Bowers and McBride — the largest single scarcity edge on the board, and it lands on your round-2 turn.

        Round-by-round plan

        Following the rb_priority strategy — your stated preference, not whichever strategy won. Availability is the probability the player is still on the board when you pick; the bar shows which position the strategy took here across all simulations.

        What the RB preference costs

        Method & caveats. The Ringer publishes no projected points, so value is derived from its auction-value column — auction dollars are themselves a value-over-replacement calculation, so AV → VOR is close to linear. Consequence:

        • Rankings, VOR gaps and tier breaks are meaningful; the absolute point totals are on a plausible-but-synthetic scale.
        • Availability odds assume your league drafts near consensus ADP. A keeper league or an early positional run will shift them.
        • The model optimizes projected season points for the starting lineup. It does not model bye conflicts, injury risk, playoff schedule, or in-season trades.
